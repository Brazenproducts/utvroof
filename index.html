Overview

UTV roofs are one of the most functional accessories you can add to a side-by-side. Without a roof, you're fully exposed to sun, rain, trail debris, and overhanging brush. A quality roof addresses all of these. The material choice defines the trade-off: hard tops (polycarbonate or aluminum) provide the best protection and rigidity; soft tops (fabric) are lighter and easier to remove; half roofs block overhead sun while maintaining airflow. Your riding climate and use case should drive the decision. Polycarbonate hard tops are the current favorite — they're rigid enough to deflect debris and protect from rain, lighter than aluminum, and provide reasonable UV protection. Installation on most major platforms requires no drilling and takes under 45 minutes.

Buying Guide — What to Know Before You Buy

Polycarbonate vs. Aluminum

Polycarbonate: lighter, cheaper, adequate for most conditions, can yellow with age. Aluminum: heavier, more expensive, better heat reflection, never yellows. Choose aluminum for extreme heat climates; polycarbonate for everywhere else.

Full Roof vs. Half Roof

Full roof: maximum protection from sun, rain, and debris. Half roof: maximum airflow with partial sun/debris protection. For wet climates, full roof wins. For hot, dry climates, half roof is often more comfortable.

Installation Complexity

Platform-specific roofs are designed to mount to factory cage tubing without drilling. Universal roofs require more fitting and measurement. Always choose platform-specific when available.

UV Protection for Polycarbonate

UV coating quality varies significantly between brands. Cheap polycarbonate tops yellow and cloud within 2–3 years. Look for products that specifically mention multi-layer UV inhibitor coating in their product description.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do UTV roofs affect the roll cage's safety rating?

Quality bolt-on roofs that mount to factory cage points don't affect structural integrity. Avoid any roof that requires cutting or welding cage tubes — this can compromise the cage's certified rating.

Can I add lighting under a UTV hard top?

Yes. Many hard tops have pre-drilled mounting points or can be drilled for interior LED light bars. This is a popular modification for night trail riding.

How do I prevent a polycarbonate top from yellowing?

UV inhibitor coating is built-in on quality tops. Additionally, apply a polycarbonate UV protectant spray (not designed for automotive plastics) every season. Avoid parking under direct sun when possible during storage.

Are UTV roofs interchangeable between brands?

No. UTV roofs are specific to machine make, model, and year. The cage tube spacing, diameter, and mounting point locations differ significantly between Polaris, Can-Am, Yamaha, and other brands.

What's the best UTV roof for riding in rain?

A full polycarbonate or aluminum hard top provides the best rain protection. Add an optional windshield for complete front protection. Soft tops offer some rain protection but can leak through seams in heavy rain.

How much does a UTV roof weigh?

Polycarbonate roofs: 12–20 lbs. Aluminum roofs: 25–40 lbs. This affects the machine's center of gravity — stay within the manufacturer's specified roof weight limit for safe handling.

Can I paint a polycarbonate UTV roof?

Not with standard automotive paint — most paints crack on flexible polycarbonate. Use paint specifically formulated for polycarbonate or polyurethane. Better option: purchase in the color you want originally.

Do UTV hard tops help with dust control?

Somewhat — a hard top reduces overhead dust intrusion and keeps the cab slightly more contained. For serious dust control, add a windshield and doors as well. A roof alone won't meaningfully reduce dust from the trail.
